I believe that in life, love, and everything that pertains to people, the blueprint is essentially the same: Acquisition, Satisfaction, and Retention. Whether we're talking about customers, women, or even business, the principles remain largely the same.

## **Acquisition** is what you do to acquire. When it comes to money, you might need to invest time, energy, or mental effort, depending on the situation. The same applies to women, whether through direct or indirect pickup, or attraction through passive and subtle means. For customers, it could be creativity, advertising, presentation, cold calling, or any other strategy you choose to get people to buy from you. They all follow the same blueprint.
Acquisition starts with two things:
## **Know your offer**: An offer is a combination of products, services, or benefits you bring to the table.
Know who is worth that offer: Understand your audience. Know what makes them tick, what they desire, and what they are looking for.

## **Satisfaction** is about doing more than you promise. Whether as a brand or as a man, the goal is to make those you have acquired happy that they chose you. This requires knowing what you can offer and then delivering it to the best of your ability.
Satisfaction varies across individuals and categories of people. Understanding who needs more, who needs intensity, and what kind of effort resonates with each person is a skill in itself. For men, satisfaction also differs from woman to woman. Although the indicators of satisfaction may be similar, the effort, approach, and skill required will differ depending on the recipient.

**Retention** is clearly the ability to keep your customers. In business, we call it the retention rate. Naturally, you would expect that satisfied customers would come back, right? After all, I hear curiosity kills the cat, but satisfaction keeps the pussy coming or cumming.

On the contrary, some don't come back. Which tells me that retention is an independent skill of its own. Satisfaction doesn't automatically guarantee loyalty, just as acquiring someone's attention doesn't guarantee their continued interest.
At the end of the day, acquisition gets them through the door, satisfaction gives them a reason to stay, but retention is what makes them come back.
