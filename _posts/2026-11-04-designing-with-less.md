Minimalism is often described as removing things until only the essential
remains. That is the wrong order. The essential part comes first, and everything
else has to justify itself against it.

<!--more-->

Most interfaces I find tiring are not ugly. They are unranked. Every element
shouts at the same volume, so the eye has no route through the page and has to
invent one. That effort is what people mean when they say a screen feels
"busy", even when it is not crowded.

## Hierarchy is the work

Before choosing a colour or a typeface, answer one question: what is this
screen for? If the answer is "reading an article", then the article wins, and
the share buttons, the newsletter banner, and the related-posts rail are all
guests in its house.

That single decision produces most of the layout. A measure of roughly
sixty-five characters. A line height that lets the eye find the next line
without hunting. A heading that is clearly louder than the body but not
theatrical about it. Whitespace that separates groups instead of decorating
them.

## Two colours are usually enough

Paper and ink will carry an entire publication. A single accent is enough for
links, for the one thing you want clicked, and for the occasional mark of
emphasis. The moment a third accent appears, the reader has to learn a language
nobody taught them.

> Restraint is not the absence of decisions. It is the visible result of making
> them early, and then not revisiting them every sprint.

This site uses one accent. It appears on links, on the current navigation item,
on the small rule beside a quote. That is the whole system, and it holds
because it is small enough to remember.

## What to remove

Not features. Assumptions.

- Remove the assumption that every page needs a sidebar.
- Remove the assumption that more content below the fold is better.
- Remove the assumption that a border is needed to separate things that are
  already separated by space.

What remains is not empty. It is quiet, which is different. Quiet leaves room
for the thing the reader came for, and it is the only kind of minimalism that
survives contact with real content.
